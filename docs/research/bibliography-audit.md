# Bibliography audit of `debate-techniques.md`

Date: 2026-11-17. Audited file: `debate-techniques.md` (366 lines), left unmodified. The audit was run on the original Spanish version of the file; line numbers (L…) refer to that audited version.

Method: every source was opened on the original's page (arXiv abstract, HTML text of the paper on arXiv or ar5iv, publisher page or cited URL). When the original was not readable, the alternative source used is stated.

Verdicts:
- **CONFIRMED**: the source exists, the metadata are correct and what is attributed matches.
- **QUALIFY**: the source exists, but the claim omits a condition, generalises or misattributes a detail.
- **INCORRECT**: the source does not exist or says something else.
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: I could not check it against the original.

## Mandatory corrections

1. **L19 (row 6):** do not attribute sycophancy to RLHF as an established cause. Sharma et al. say "probably caused in part" by human preferences.
2. **L69, L281 and L309 (rows 13, 25 and 38):** tone down what is attributed to Zheng et al. Self-preference is inconclusive according to the authors themselves, and the verbosity bias barely affects GPT-4 (8.7%).
3. **L302 (row 31):** clarify that 48%/60% is the baseline with no expert at all, not consultancy (54%/78%).
4. **L306 (row 35):** split the findings between the two papers and change "the most effective" to "the tactic most associated with collapse". Neither of them measures debate between agents.
5. **L308 (row 37):** correct "even with debate instructions". The abstract says those instructions increase inconstancy.
6. **L173 (row 16):** name Wagemans (2011) as the author of the secondary source behind the Springer DOI.
7. **L17 (row 4):** rule 6 does not require fixing criteria beforehand; cite the opening stage.
8. **L176-180 and L200 (rows 18 and 20):** keep or add the label "unverified in the original" and rename "cause to effect" as "correlation to cause".
